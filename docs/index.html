We gave the busywork to a machine.

JETAASC is run entirely by volunteers with day jobs. Every hour spent reformatting the same event announcement for the sixth time is an hour not spent on the events themselves. Here is what we automated, and what it gave back.

The workload

Where the hours actually went

Four recurring jobs consumed most of the board's administrative time. None of them were hard. All of them were repetitive, and all of them had to happen every single month.

The core problem

One event. Six destinations.

A single dinner has to reach the website, the calendar, Discord, the RSVP page, the newsletter, and sometimes Facebook. Each one wants the description worded differently and the flyer sized differently. Done by hand, the same event gets rebuilt six times.

By handsix separate rebuilds
With automationone pass, one review

The work that disappeared is the copying, resizing, and reformatting. The work that stayed is deciding what we want to say, and approving it before it goes out.

Compounding effect

The newsletter went from a day to an afternoon

The monthly newsletter was the single biggest drain: chasing contributions, formatting them, resizing flyers, then rebuilding the whole email. Now the board contributes to one shared document all month, and that document becomes the email.

Nobody has to remember the deadline, the flyer naming convention, or the artist credit line. That knowledge lives in the process now instead of in one person's head, which also means the job survives a change in board membership.

Controls

Speed without giving up the wheel

A chapter's credibility is its mailing list and its word. Automation is only worth it if a mistake cannot reach members unreviewed. These limits are built into the process itself.

Nothing sends itselfEmail is drafted and left waiting. A board member presses send, every time.
Nothing gets inventedAn unknown date or venue stays marked TBA rather than being filled in with something plausible.
Nothing is promisedNo speaking slot, role, or commitment offered on the board's behalf.
Nothing untested goes publicNew behavior is trialled in a private admin channel members cannot see.
The takeaway

The real return was writing it down

The hours are the headline. The durable win is quieter.

To automate any of this, we first had to state plainly how JETAASC actually operates: what a Boba Banter announcement should sound like, which deadline the newsletter runs on, where the flyers live, who reviews what. That had never been written down. It lived in the heads of whoever happened to be doing the job.

Now it is written down. The automation is useful, and it will keep saving roughly 6 hours a month. But the documented process is what makes the next board's job easier, with or without any of this.
