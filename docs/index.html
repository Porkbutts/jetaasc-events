// how the chapter actually runs

Three pipelines do the work nobody volunteered for.

JETAASC is run by volunteers with day jobs. Publishing an event used to mean rebuilding it six times by hand. Now it moves through a pipeline: one input, one review, five destinations. Here is each pipeline, and what it gives back.

Pipeline 01

Publishing an event

One event has to reach the website, the calendar, Discord, the RSVP page, and sometimes Facebook. Each wants different wording and a different image format. The pipeline does that shaping once, and the board approves once.

The flyer is the part that used to cause the most rework. It now gets uploaded once to a shared folder, and every channel pulls the format it needs from that one file.

Pipeline 02

The monthly newsletter

The newsletter was the biggest drain: chasing contributions, formatting them, resizing flyers, then rebuilding the whole email. Now the board contributes to one document all month, and that document becomes the email.

The deadline, the flyer naming convention, and the artist credit line used to live in one person's head. They live in the pipeline now, which means the job survives a change in board membership.

Pipeline 03

Answering the inbox

Volunteers, prospective speakers, venues, and returning JETs all write in. Each reply needs to know what we actually have going on. That research is the slow part, and it is the part that got automated.

Note where this one ends. The draft sits in Gmail until a board member reads it and presses send. No pipeline at JETAASC reaches a member without a person in front of it.

Controls

Every pipeline stops for a human

A chapter's credibility is its mailing list and its word. These limits are built into the pipelines themselves, not left to whoever is running one.

Nothing sends itselfEmail is drafted and left waiting. A board member presses send.
Nothing gets inventedAn unknown date or venue stays marked TBA rather than filled in with something plausible.
Nothing is promisedNo speaking slot, role, or commitment offered on the board's behalf.
Nothing untested goes publicNew behaviour is trialled in a private admin channel members cannot see.
The takeaway

Building the pipeline meant writing down how we work

The hours are the headline. The durable win is quieter.

To build any of this we first had to state plainly how JETAASC operates: what a Boba Banter announcement should sound like, which deadline the newsletter runs on, where the flyers live, who reviews what. None of it had ever been written down. It lived in the heads of whoever happened to be doing the job.

Now it is written down, and the pipelines follow it. That is what makes the next board's job easier, with or without any of the automation.
